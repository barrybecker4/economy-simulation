# Lab: Prices

**Link:** `?lesson=prices`

**Question:** Which prices rise while others fall?

## This lab cannot show

- This lab is one closed economy. There is no trade with other countries.
- A category price that rises does not mean people switched stores; it is an accounting split of one basket.
- The result depends on the lever you change. A different assumption can reverse the picture.

## 15-minute script

1. Open the link. The baseline run starts on its own (about two minutes on a laptop).
2. Ask students to pick a guess before they read the snapshot: which categories get cheaper?
3. When charts appear, point at CPI, housing, medical care, apparel, and electronics.
4. Read the snapshot and the typical-household paragraph together.
5. Ask: if apparel got cheaper and housing got more expensive, what happened to a paycheck that buys both?

## Student prompts

- Which line fell the most?
- Did the CPI tell the whole story?

## Seed-1 answer key (120 months, registry population)

- Apparel ends lower than the opening.
- Electronics ends lower than the opening.
- Housing ends higher than the opening.
- Matching guess: “Apparel and electronics get cheaper.”
