# Lab: Interest

**Link:** `?lesson=interest`

**Question:** Who sets the interest rate, and what happens to jobs?

## This lab cannot show

- This lab is one closed economy. There is no trade with other countries.
- A category price that rises does not mean people switched stores; it is an accounting split of one basket.
- The result depends on the lever you change. A different assumption can reverse the picture.

## 15-minute script

1. Open the link. The balanced central-bank baseline runs and pins itself.
2. Ask for a guess: if the central bank fights inflation harder, what happens to jobs at the end?
3. Switch the lever to Hawkish, keep the guess, and press Run.
4. Read the snapshot: on seed 1 the policy rate ends lower and unemployment ends about the same as the balanced path.
5. Optional: try Employment-leaning. Unemployment and the policy rate both end a little higher than balanced.

## Student prompts

- Why might the job line barely move when the rate rule changes?
- What else would you need to change to see a larger job effect?

## Seed-1 answer key (120 months, registry population)

- Hawkish versus Balanced: unemployment ends the same; the policy rate ends lower.
- Employment-leaning versus Balanced: unemployment ends higher; the policy rate ends higher.
- Matching guess for Hawkish: “Unemployment stays about the same.”
