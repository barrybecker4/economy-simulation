# Lab: Interest

**Link:** `?lesson=interest`

**Question:** What does a stronger inflation response do when inflation is already low?

## This lab cannot show

- This lab is one closed economy. There is no trade with other countries.
- A category price that rises does not mean people switched stores; it is an accounting split of one basket.
- The result depends on the lever you change. A different assumption can reverse the picture.
- Each month the bank sets the policy interest rate with a formula that adds a few pieces. One piece is: multiplier × (inflation − 2%). Add that number (positive or negative) into the rate. Under 2% → lower rate; over 2% → higher rate. A larger multiplier makes that push bigger.
- Jobs barely move in this lab, so this lesson is only about the interest rate.

## Plain numbers (for the teacher)

The full fiat rule (simplified) is:

`policy rate ≈ other pieces + multiplier × (inflation − 2%)`

(clamped so it cannot go below 0%). The “other pieces” cover a neutral real rate and current inflation; this lab only changes the multiplier.

- Baseline multiplier: **1.5**
- Change: multiplier **1.5 → 2.5**
- Example if inflation were 0% and the goal 2%:  
  - Baseline: add `1.5 × (−2%) = −3` percentage points into the rate  
  - Stronger: add `2.5 × (−2%) = −5` percentage points (a bigger cut)
- So “react more strongly” means **a larger add-in on the miss**, which **cuts** when inflation is too low and **raises** when it is too high. It does not mean “always raise rates.”

## 15-minute script

1. Open the link. Point at Inflation: the line stays under 0.02.
2. Write on the board: the bank **sets** the policy rate; one add-in is multiplier × (inflation − 2%).
3. Ask the prediction with the 1.5 → 2.5 numbers in the prompt.
4. Students press **Try: Use a larger inflation multiplier (1.5 → 2.5)**.
5. Confirm the dashed policy rate ends lower. Stress: the everyday word “hawkish” misleads when inflation is already low.

## Student prompts

- What does the formula output — a guess, a recommendation, or the policy interest rate itself?
- On this chart, is the inflation add-in positive or negative?
- If inflation were 4% instead, would the same larger multiplier raise or cut the rate?

## Seed-1 answer key (120 months, registry population)

- Inflation stays below 2% every month.
- Multiplier 2.5 versus 1.5: the policy rate ends lower.
- Matching guess: “Lower than the baseline.”
